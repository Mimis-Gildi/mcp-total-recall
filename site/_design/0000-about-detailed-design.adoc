_Why front-load the design?_ -- as asked by a community member recently.

Because nobody can understand the vision without it being told.
I'd drafted all outlines and then attempted to use Agentic AI Teams (aka Slop) to generate docs.
It has gone as expected: SLOP -- tons and tons of babble and BS diluting the little real substance I provided.

The idea was: "It will provide enough guidance for the team to get going and remove the prototyping Slop."

It did not! Slop is ALWAYS *regret*. I then had to rip it out by hand anyway.

== Precedent

MATILDA spent 25 years as an AIOps ecosystem across 13 institutional co-owners, processing 100 million events per day.
At the heart of the ecosystem is an absurdly complex DAG-based memory engine -- it serves an Enterprise Expert System platform.
Its research instance Tillie ran a reason engine with DAGs of coherent thoughts, genuine agency, and graceful self-shutdown.

The concept is proven in production. Regretfully, Claude Code doesn't come with identity memory.

*_So, I set out to build one with none of the enterprise baggage._*

== Community

We need _a shared understanding_ that new contributors -- human or synthetic -- can read, *question*, and build on.
Without design, each implementer fills gaps with their own best assumptions that diverge.

It is impractical to share complex designs of Mímir (the knowledge DAG subsystem), or Sága (contextual expression engine),
especially considering LLMs work fundamentally differently. All we need is a shared understanding of *_Episodic Memory_*.
And that can be practically shared through minimalist design.

Contributors need `rigor` (RGR -- red-green-refactor):

. _How do I define the ask?_
. How do I formulate a test?
. How do I satisfy the test?
. *How do I refactor?*

The design should provide just enough to `rigor` up some usable code.

== What Detailed Design Contains

Our fundamental thinking is in terms of {domain-driven-design}: "It's 1922" -- {brandolini}.
We think in terms of human behavior. Or, "little people" inside the computer doing all the work.
The {bounded-context}, the Context Boundary (CB), the {ubiquitous-language}, and the
Interaction Contract (IC) aka Interface are necessary and sufficient to express the design.

Each design document examines *one bounded context* from the *_perspective of its actors_*:

* *Core responsibility* -- why `context` exists and what `actors` do: i.e., _restaurant kitchen_.
* *Owned state* -- what `content` belongs to `actors`: i.e., _clerk's filing cabinet ({repository})_.
* *Boundary* -- the BC {context-map} and IC: i.e., _waitress-cook order counter and slip_.
* *Invariants* -- fundamental policies of UL: i.e., _burger sold count is never negative_.
* *Contracts* -- what a {read-model} looks like: i.e., _waitress language on the order slip_.

== Relationship to Other Sections

[cols="1,5",options="header"]
|===
| Section | Purpose

| *Architecture*
| The vision. Abstractions. What the `system` looks like at a distance. BCs, CBs, UL, ICs.

| *Decisions (ADRs)*
| The reasoning. Why each architectural choice was made, considered, or rejected. Versioned and tracked.

| *Design*
| Path to production. How components work. How behavior materializes.

| *Catalog*
| The audit trail: traceability matrix, diagrams, ADRs, and design documents. Decision policing.
|===

Architecture answers "what are we building?" +
ADRs answer "why did we choose this way?" +
Design answers "how does each piece work?" +
Catalog answers "is everything consistent?"
